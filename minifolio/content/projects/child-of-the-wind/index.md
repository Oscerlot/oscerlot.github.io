>Navigate your oriental ship safely across the many table-top seas using two hand-fans to influence the wind. Steer enemy ships into ruins and avoid natural dangers, including thrashing whirlpools while collecting your cargo and bringing it to safety in a beautiful, Japanese-inspired environment. 

- Child of The Wind was my first commercial game release, and was made over a period of 5 months with a team of four, and contracted a custom soundtrack.
- It didn't meet a huge audience, as it was fairly niche at the time, even for early VR, which found a lot more attention in shooting, sports, and simulation titles. However, those who did like it, absolutely loved it.
- A key pillar of the game was to ensure that every action in the game was diegetic, even where if a player wanted to raise up the main table, there was a crank under it that they had to wind to move the table up and down to a comfortable height.
- We wanted to really make the mechanical interactions feel connected with the game environment also, so obstacles were designed to have logical interactions with the player.
  - For instance, closed gates could be opened to allow the ship to pass if the player used their hand-fans to create a gust of wind on a nearby fan that powered the gate and opened it.
