Fitbit Ace Arcade

11 Games created for the Ace Arcade on the Fitbit Ace LTE, a fitness powered wearable for kids!

What is it?

The Fitbit Ace LTE is a device for kids, where being active is rewarded with game-time. We developed a portfolio of 11 games in Unity for the device to date, with additional games developed by Tantalus Media, Mighty Games, and Robot Circus.

Many of our (best) games are still yet to launch, but the ones that have been released include:

So what did I actually do?

I was primarily responsible for leading the team, and the creative design of the games, as well as engaging with the client and making sure they're loving what we're giving them around the gameplay, and IP alignment.

As we needed to be developing two games in parallel, keeping the scope tight throughout the project was a key requirement, while still adapting to what we found gave players the most value in play. This also included setting our specific milestone requirements, roadmapping, and epic, task, and story-point management, to make sure we're hitting our targets.

In additional to my general design and production responsabilities, a brief list of additional duties across the life of these projects includes:

  • Prototype development
  • Localization implementation
  • UI implementation
  • Developing key features, and technical art systems (C#)
  • On-device debugging via Android Studio, and bug fixing
  • Liasing with translators, and providing transliteration notes
  • Release management, and deliverables via Gerrit
  • QA / LQA
  • Writing game cheats for partner QA team
  • Essentially at points I've done a bit of everything you could do while making a game... except actual art, definitely not art...

Notable Info

Since I can't go into everything, here are some of the more unqiue, but general elements of these projects:

Playtime

The playtime requirements of these games needed to follow, what I like to call, a 5-4-90 rule. That being that kids should play:

  • 5 minute game sessions,
  • 4 times per day,
  • for 90 days

So essentially each of these games needed to have 30 hours of content, which was TOUGH to design and plan for sometimes, expecially with the 6 month turnaround.

Gameplay and Sensors

A tenent of the gameplay for all games was to integrate the device sensors in meaningful ways. That being the gyro, altimeter, accelerometer, magnometer, light, and touch.

This ranged from being simple, but, core game features, as in the case of Zoomies, where you tilt the watch to control the character movement.

Then extended to more micro-game-esque in Gnome Heisters, where you trained the Gnomes for your Heist by completing a series of goofy, several-second-long challenges, such as blowing on the screen to extinguish the burning Gnome.

Custom Tooling

Because the watch was propriatary, and had a custom SDK to interface with, getting the team to be always playing builds on-device was a challenge, expecially for the less technically inclined members who did not enjoy using a terminal to install builds, or pull logs.

To minimize the friction, I developed an in-house GUI tool to allow the team to one-click install builds to their device from a remote build-agent, start the games, take screenshots, pull logs, etc.

This majorly improved the on-device testing throughout the team, and we got much better buy-in from the team on the project overall, better feedback, and faster turnaround of device-related features.
