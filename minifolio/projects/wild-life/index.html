Wild Life: Puzzle Story

Play bubble-shooter levels and help Chloe save the farm!

What's the Game?

  • An original IP game, blending bubble-shooter gameplay with a narrative layer, which was a novel integration at the time in a space that was dominated by Match-3.

  • The story follows Chloe, as she returns to her family farm, planning on selling it, only to find out it's being purchased to make way for a new highway!

    • You'll meet furry friends, in particular Kit, your companion fox, who will help you on your journey to restore the farm.

  • Complete bubble-shooter levels to earn stars to spend doing tasks and progress the story.

  • A unique narrative engine was developed for the game, which helped us lock down several future projects, including Ava's Manor, Star Trek: TBD, and Power Rangers: Mighty Force.

So what did I actually do?

  • Game Design, Level Design, Narrative Design, and pivoted into Product Management on the project after launch, to lead the game direction.
  • Personally created over 1,000 levels
  • Wrote the narrative and dialogue for the initial game launch
    • I later guided the design team in expanding the narrative and dialogue for future updates
  • Designed the live ops events, including seasonal, with unique mechanics such as uncovering comic panels to discover a unique animal story as you play
  • Worked with international contractors to produce additional game levels, with a feedback loop to balance quality vs quantity
  • Worked with the backend team to create a propriatary backend system (MKNet) for managing users, events, live ops, updates.
    • This was then used in future MK titles, and further developed to support the other major titles
  • Worked with existing PM to secure a publisher, Rogue Games
  • Released a version of the game in China
  • Designed the analytics events with the Backend team, and analyzed in Tableau
  • Designed and managed live ops events, including AB Testing, optimizing for conversion
  • Updated levels based on level funnel metrics
  • Responsible for KPIs (Retention, ARPU, LTV, CVR, RoAS, CVR) etc.

Brief retro

  • While the game still performed decently in the market, and really set us up for future narrative projects that paid off, it didn't do as well as we originally hoped when comparing ourselves to similar narrative titles that used a Match-3 game mechanic instead.
    • I primarily attribute this to the inherent limitation of a bubble-shooter being unable to provide the same level of calculated randomness as a Match-3 does and encourages players to spend and activate boosts to complete a level.
    • Since it's a puzzle game at it's core, rather than more akin to a slots game like a Match-3 is, most players are driven by wanting to solve the puzzle of it, and will often only spend when they feel the level isn't possible without doing so, which is an unfair experience to give.
